# ClaimPilot voice-over script (~3:00)

Read each scene into its **own audio file**, named `scene1.m4a` … `scene9.m4a`, saved in `audio_raw_files/`.
Leave **one second of silence** at the start and end of every clip. Calm pace, short pause at each full stop.
Don't worry about timing: the screen recording is cut to fit your audio. A fluff? Just re-record that one scene.

---

**Scene 1 — Introduction** (~15 s)
> Hi, I'm Divyam. This is ClaimPilot, an AI assistant for expense claims, built for the Jio AI Innovation Lab. In the next three minutes, I'll show you how it turns a pile of bills into a claim that's ready to submit.

**Scene 2 — The problem** (~15 s)
> First, the problem. Filing an expense claim takes fifteen to twenty-five minutes. You collect bills, type in every detail, and guess what the policy allows. And even then, Finance often sends it back for a missing GST bill or a duplicate.

**Scene 3 — Adding the bills** (~20 s)
> So let's see how ClaimPilot handles it. Here is a two-day client trip to Mumbai: cab rides, meals, a hotel bill with its UPI payment, a handwritten fuel bill, an office supplies bill, and money sent to a colleague. I add all thirteen at once, and the AI starts reading every bill and filling in the details by itself.

**Scene 4 — Clear reasons for every decision** (~30 s)
> Now, reading is only half the job. For every value, I can see exactly where on the bill it was read from. But the AI only reads. The decisions are made by fixed company rules. That's why the hotel's UPI payment is matched to its bill, so it isn't counted twice. The office supplies bill is stopped, because its GST number is not valid. Money sent to a person is not allowed. And when a meal goes over the limit, "Why?" shows the exact line from the company policy.

**Scene 5 — Only the questions that matter** (~25 s)
> So what's left for the employee? Very little. For thirteen bills, it asks only three questions: a date it can't read, a cab bill uploaded twice, and a reason for the costly meal. If I just type "no", it isn't accepted, because that explains nothing. A proper answer goes through. After one last question about the fuel trip, the claim is ready.

**Scene 6 — Submit, or ask for a review** (~20 s)
> Now I send it to Finance, and I get a reference number straight away. And if a bill was rejected and I think that's wrong, I'm not stuck. I can raise a ticket, and a person in Finance will review it, along with my note.

**Scene 7 — The Finance team's view** (~20 s)
> That's the employee's side. Now let's switch to the Finance team. They get a live dashboard: how many claims needed no questions at all, the cost per claim in rupees, the time saved, and the money saved by catching duplicates. All of it is measured from every claim the system processes.

**Scene 8 — Can we trust it?** (~25 s)
> But how do we know it's right? We tested it on sixty-eight sample Indian bills: blurry, tilted, handwritten, and some uploaded twice. It got every detail, every duplicate and every policy check right. Every change is re-tested automatically. And the policy itself is written in plain English, then turned into rules, each linked to the exact policy line.

**Scene 9 — Close** (~15 s)
> To sum up: ClaimPilot saves employees time, saves Finance from rework, and explains every decision it makes. It isn't tied to any one AI company, and the online demo runs without any paid keys. ClaimPilot: from a pile of bills to a claim that's ready to submit. Thank you.
